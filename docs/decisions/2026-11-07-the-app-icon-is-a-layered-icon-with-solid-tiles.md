# 2026-11-07 · The app icon is a layered icon with solid tiles

- **Decision:** Both apps use one layered Icon Composer icon, `Shared/AppIcon.icon`, in place of the PNG icon sets. It keeps the 2026-11-05 mark: two clinking tiles with a `water` and a `shot` bubble. The bubbles are glass with some translucency. The tiles keep the glass edge and highlight but no translucency. In tinted, the `beer` tile and the `shot` bubble are dimmed so the shapes stay apart. `scripts/make-app-icons.swift` still writes the whole icon, now as SVG layers and the icon's JSON, and `scripts/test.sh lint` checks its colors against `Shared/Palette.swift`.
- **Why:** A layered icon gets the system's Liquid Glass look and the clear look, which flat PNGs can't. With full translucency, `fizz` showed through the cream tile in light, and the tiles went brick and mustard in dark, against The Content Stays Solid Rule in `DESIGN.md`, so the tiles stay solid. Tinted icons lose their hues, and the two tiles merged into one shade until one was dimmed. Keeping the script means the colors can't drift from the palette, and changing the icon is an edit, not new artwork.
- **Instead of:** Drawing it in the Icon Composer app, which leaves no check on the colors. Adding a + for "add an entry", tried three ways and dropped: on the water bubble it was too small to read at 40pt, in place of the shot bubble it said "add a drink", and cut into a tile it read as a first-aid cross. A separate Debug icon, which only helps once Debug builds have their own bundle ID.
